# Video script — Ledger (2 min 55 s)

**Rules of the contest, applied here:** under three minutes, public on YouTube, no music, no
third-party trademark on screen (all statements, donors and beneficiaries are fictional), the
project shown **on the device it was built for** (a phone, 390×844), and the strongest shot inside
the first twenty seconds.

**How to film:** screen recording (Playwright, `scripts/smoke.mjs` already drives the pages) with a
voice-over recorded separately. Two framings only: **desktop 1440×900** for the repository, the
terminal and the proofs; **phone 390×844** for the two applications. Never a picture of a picture.

**Numbers on screen must be the measured ones** — they are all regenerated by
`npx vite-node scripts/recette.ts` and written to `docs/recette.json`.

---

## 0:00 → 0:12 — the question (12 s)

*Shot:* phone, dark, 6:40 in the morning. A bank notification slides in. Cut to the app: one large
figure, nothing else.

> "Thursday, 26 November, six in the morning. Sarah opens her phone and the only question she has
> is this: can I still spend tomorrow? Three thousand lines of bank statements, and no tool on
> earth answers that without a server, an account, and a spreadsheet."

## 0:12 → 0:30 — the household (18 s)

*Shot:* phone, the household app (`/envelopes/`). Drop a statement file; the import wizard shows
the detection — `;`, decimal comma, Windows-1252 — then a confirm; the grid fills; the single
number appears at the top.

> "Here is the answer, and it is one number. Drop a statement in: the app reads three years of
> twelve thousand lines in half a second — five hundred and four milliseconds — detects the
> separator, the decimal comma and the encoding before it imports a single row. Correct one
> assignment, it learns the rule. Leftovers roll over. And nothing was uploaded: the page made
> zero network requests, and I can prove it."

*(cut: the "0 foreign request" line of the proof page)*

## 0:30 → 0:48 — the law (18 s)

*Shot:* desktop. The repository, `packages/core/src/journal.ts`. A cursor tries to change an
amount — the console shows the frozen-object error. Then `contra()`: the reversal appears next to
the original, the original's hash unchanged. Then `verifyChain()` — `ok`.

> "This is the whole engine: one law — every entry sums to zero — and one rule: you cannot edit an
> entry. You reverse it. The mistake stays visible forever, and the hash chain proves nothing was
> rewritten. Correctness here is not an opinion. It is an equation, so it can be tested instead of
> argued about."

## 0:48 → 1:10 — the fund (22 s)

*Shot:* phone, the fund app (`/mutual-aid/`). A donation entry. The publish screen: private side
and public side, side by side — "a household, case 41" instead of a name. The public page opens.
A donor types a receipt: `verified`.

> "The same core, the opposite promise. This is a solidarity fund: six volunteers, a hundred and
> eighty donors. Every donation is published in plain language, beneficiaries become case numbers,
> and any donor can check a receipt against the chain themselves — offline, with no tool, no
> account. One question answered: where did the money go?"

## 1:10 → 1:30 — one core, two apps (20 s)

*Shot:* desktop, the two `apps/` folders side by side; then the purity guard refusing a
concept that belongs to an application; then the budget guard.

> "Two applications that share everything and disagree about everything: one hides all of it, one
> publishes all of it. And a guard that says no. This concept belongs to an application, not to
> the skeleton — it names the owner. The skeleton is two thousand one hundred and twenty-nine lines
> out of three thousand two hundred. The ceiling has never moved: the only accepted fix is to
> delete code."

## 1:30 → 1:50 — the unknown room (20 s)

*Shot:* the `docs/UNKNOWN-ROOM.md` page, the clock, the time-bank app running.

> "The test I wanted: hand this repository to someone who has never opened it, with one page of
> brief. A time bank — hours given and received, a unit that is not a currency — no envelope, one
> monthly report. Sixteen minutes and thirty-two seconds later: a working application, five hundred
> and forty-two lines, and not one line changed inside the core."

## 1:50 → 2:15 — the proofs (25 s)

*Shot:* the terminal running `scripts/recette.ts`, the numbers appearing one by one; then the
seed sweep; then the proof page.

> "Every number on this video comes from one command. Twelve thousand lines imported in five
> hundred and four milliseconds. Trial balance: five million eight hundred and fifteen thousand
> three hundred and ninety-six on each side. Twelve thousand links verified in a hundred and
> eighty-one milliseconds. Tamper with one byte and the chain tells you exactly where: sequence
> four thousand two hundred and eighty-seven, bad hash. Ninety-five tests, fifteen properties run
> ten thousand times each, plus twenty extra seeds: all green. Zero accessibility violations,
> zero network requests."

## 2:15 → 2:40 — what it does not prove (25 s)

*Shot:* the public page, the paragraph "what this chain proves / does not prove"; then the
`KIRO_JOURNAL.md` section "what did not work".

> "And the part most projects hide: what this chain does not prove. It does not prove the entries
> are sincere — somebody can book a donation that never happened, and the chain will confirm it
> forever. It does not prove the tail was not cut off. We print that limit on the public page
> itself, because the honest limit is the argument."

## 2:40 → 2:55 — the addresses (15 s)

*Shot:* the three addresses on a plain background, no decoration, no music.

> "Ledger. A skeleton you can audit. Two thousand one hundred and twenty-nine lines, one law, and
> two applications that disagree about everything except the truth. The repository, the
> applications, and the proofs that back every number you just saw."
